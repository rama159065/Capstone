About the source: fund filings on EDGAR

US mutual funds and ETFs are registered investment companies. A fund company (Vanguard, Fidelity, BlackRock and so on) groups its funds into trusts, and each trust files with the SEC on EDGAR for all of its funds; one trust can hold hundreds of funds, and each fund several share classes with their own tickers. The fund company, usually through a filing agent, prepares and uploads the documents; the SEC doesn't write them or vouch for the numbers.

FormWhat it isUse for RAG
N-CSRThe certified annual shareholder report. Since July 2024 it starts with a tailored shareholder report for each share class: expenses, a performance line graph, average annual returns, and holdings breakdown charts. Then come the full financial statements and the schedule of every holding.The main corpus: tables, charts and figures. Selected here as the core tier.

These documents are HTML, not PDF. All 500 selected main documents are .htm files (0 PDF files across all the selected filings). Fund PDFs exist on fund companies' websites: section 5 checks 84 families and finds direct PDF links for 27 of them. EDGAR is the source that covers every fund company and can be fetched in bulk. Charts are separate image files in each filing. Key figures (expense ratios, returns, net assets) are tagged as inline XBRL, so they can be read exactly and used to check RAG answers.

2. Content: tables, charts and images

Measured from each filing's file list, without opening the documents:

  • Annual reports: median 10.2 MB of HTML (largest 141 MB); 300 of 300 include chart images, 16,634 in total. The financial statements and holdings schedules are HTML tables, often thousands of rows for an index fund.

HTML tables are an advantage over PDF: rows and columns can be read exactly with pandas.read_html or BeautifulSoup, with no table detection and no VLM. The VLM is needed only for the chart images.

4. How to download them

  1. Tiers. Core: 200 annual reports (5.8 GB). Extended: the other 100 annual reports and 200 summary prospectuses, for 500 documents and 7.6 GB.
  2. What to fetch per filing: the main .htm and its image files (named in index.json). Skip the R*.htm viewer pages, the XBRL zip and the schema files.
  3. SEC rules: a User-Agent with a name and e-mail, at most 10 requests a second (8 is safe), and a back-off on 429 or 503. Resume by skipping complete files, write to .part and rename, and record the SHA-256.
  4. Manifest: one row per document with trust CIK, accession, form, filing date, category, family, the series it covers, URL, bytes and SHA-256, in the format of the existing pdf_manifest.jsonl with source_type set to fund_report_html.
  5. Split before indexing. A trust's annual report holds dozens of funds. Split it into one section per fund (the inline XBRL tags name the series and class), so each chunk carries its fund's name and ticker and retrieval doesn't mix funds.
  6. Keep the facts. Extract the inline XBRL figures (expense ratio, returns, net assets, holdings counts) into a table. That gives exact ground truth for evaluation, as the XBRL portal did in notebook 08.

Plan for 500 PDFs

  1. Direct-link families first. Their pages list 1,168 PDFs, more than the target, so 200–500 PDFs can come from them alone. Cap each family (for example 40 PDFs) and take a mix of types (annual and semi-annual reports, summary prospectuses, fact sheets, commentaries) so a few large sites don't dominate.
  2. Cover the biggest families through EDGAR. Vanguard, Fidelity, BlackRock and others don't expose plain PDF links to an automated client. Their regulatory documents are the same on EDGAR (sections 1–3), so use those rather than scraping their sites.
  3. Downloader rules. Obey robots.txt, one request a second per site, a User-Agent that names the project, stop on 403 or 429, and check each site's terms of use before bulk downloading. Record the source URL, date fetched and SHA-256 in the manifest with source_type set to fund_document_pdf.
  4. Avoid duplicates. A summary prospectus or annual report from a fund website is the same document as on EDGAR. Match on fund and date and keep one copy: the PDF for visual pipelines, the EDGAR HTML for exact tables.

  2. Use Approach 1 as the main path: HTML text and tables into Chroma (or the Qdrant text collection), and the VLM on chart images only. It is accurate on tables and cheap, and fits well inside a ₹5,000 budget.
